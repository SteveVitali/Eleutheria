# P06.1 — Vertical-slice retrospective: what the design got wrong on real data

**Slice jurisdiction:** Oklahoma City, Oklahoma — the OKCPD Flock Safety ALPR
program (Master Agreement C241032). **As-of:** 2026-09-01. **Ticket:**
`docs/tickets/P06.1__vertical-slice.md` (HARD SYNCHRONIZATION GATE, §54 /
SIG-ENG-034).

This is the artifact the hard gate exists for. Phase 6 carries **one real
jurisdiction end to end — evidence → claims → reconciliation → a rendered
dossier, and the J-1 traversal — to *falsify the design before it is replicated
twenty thousand times*** (§51.1). Below is what the slice actually broke, ordered
by how much it should change the design. Per §54, no ticket after P06.1 may begin
until this is committed.

## What worked (so the failures are legible)

- The **epistemic weight model** (§10.6, SIG-EPIS-021), implemented for the first
  time in `reconcile/weight.py`, reproduces the spec's own Appendix D.2 worked
  case **exactly**: contract → `contracted_device_count` W4; portal-style
  first-party statement → `active_device_count` W3; the same contract as evidence
  for the active count → W1; OSM mapping → W2.
- The **`PREDICATE_CONFLATION` guard** (§29.1, SIG-RECON-028) fired on the
  deliberate conflation of OKC's contracted count (90) with its mapped count
  (299), and the reconciler genuinely **refused to emit a single true count**
  (SIG-RECON-029).
- J-1 executed end to end across six independent source families, and every
  material figure carries its reconciliation and a document at a locator.

## What the design got wrong

### 1. The predicate registry shipped with half of §29.1 missing — a slice could not have run without a fix

§29.1 names **six** count predicates. Only **three** (`contracted_`, `active_`,
`installed_device_count`) existed in the committed predicate registry
(`ontology/vocab/predicates.yaml`); `invoiced_`, `mapped_`, and
`claimed_device_count` were **spec text only**. Currency `C` is derived from a
predicate's registry volatility/half-life (§28.3), so the reconciler *could not
derive `C`* for three of the six predicates the ticket requires — including
`mapped_device_count`, which the OKC slice is built around (DeFlock's 299). This
slice adds the three missing predicates (additive, back-compatible). **Finding:**
"every predicate has volatility, strategy, and a directness row" (SIG-ONTO-067)
was enforced *for predicates that exist*, but nothing enforced that the predicates
§29.1 depends on **were actually created**. A conformance check should assert the
§29.1 count-predicate set is present.

### 2. Appendix D.2's own numbers don't follow from §10.6 without an unstated upgrade

Appendix D.2 publishes OSM-mapped as `R5 · D3 · I1 · C1 → W2`. Computed straight
from the §10.6 ordinal table that is **W1** (`R5`→W2 base, `D3` −1). The only way
to recover the published **W2** is the *"machine-readable structured export AND
extraction confidence EXACT"* `+1` upgrade — which Appendix D.2 never shows. A
careful implementer following §10.6 alone gets a different answer than the spec's
own worked example. We implemented the upgrade and anchored a test on it
(`test_appendix_d2_worked_example_reproduces_exact_weight_classes`), but the
**spec should make the upgrade explicit in D.2**, or the example is quietly wrong.

### 3. The count model conflates *basis* with *scope*, and real data breaks the pipeline ordering

The Appendix-D worked example assumes `contracted ≥ active ≥ mapped`: the deltas
read as "were some never installed?" and "go map the unmapped ones." **Oklahoma
City inverts it.** `mapped_device_count` (299, DeFlock, metro-wide, includes
business/HOA cameras) is far *larger* than `active_device_count` (90,
police-operated, city). The −209 gap is not "devices to go map"; it is "devices
with a **non-city or unknown operator**" (§29.2 attribution). The generic delta
interpreter had to special-case the sign. **Finding:** a count predicate needs a
**scope/population** dimension (whose devices, over what area) in addition to its
`count_basis`. Without it, `contracted (city)` and `mapped (metro)` look
comparable and their delta is meaningless. §29.1 treats "what is counted" and
"whose/where it is counted" as one axis; they are two.

### 4. `count_basis` is load-bearing in §29.1 but is not a field anywhere

The Phase-2.3 guard keys `PREDICATE_CONFLATION` on claims that "disagree on
`count_basis`" — yet `count_basis` is **not a column on `claim`, not a slot in
the ontology, and not on the generated `Claim` model**. In practice the predicate
*is* the basis, so the slice derives one from the other. The design must decide:
is `count_basis` derived from `predicate_id`, or a stored field? Leaving it
implicit means the guard's own vocabulary has no home in the schema.

### 5. The generated `Contradiction` / `ResearchTask` models cannot represent a real contradiction

`db/deploy/graph_annotations.sql` has `contradiction_type`, `claim_ids`,
`severity`, `status`; the **generated Pydantic `Contradiction`** has only
`subject`, `predicate`, `state`, `id` — no `contradiction_type`. The slice had to
define its own value objects (`reconcile/model.py`) to carry a
`policy_configuration_divergence` or a `value_disagreement` at all. The
contradiction-type vocabulary (§31, nine codes) is **spec-only** — not in any
generated vocabulary or SQL seed. The ontology should generate these, or every
downstream consumer reinvents them (as this slice did).

### 6. "Executable end to end against real data" is only half-true at Phase 6, by construction

Phase 6 is sequenced **before** the records/procurement/parsing connectors (P07),
the reconciliation engine (P08), the portal layer (P11), and the API/web surfaces
(P14/P15). There is therefore **no connector that can fetch a contract PDF, a
transparency portal, or a council packet** when the slice runs. The slice's
"real data" is committed fixtures faithfully transcribing cited public sources
(URLs recorded as `stable_locator`), captured as of 2026-09-01 — not live
captures. This is an honest tension in the phase plan: the phase whose job is to
prove J-1 on real data runs before the machinery that would *acquire* that data.
The slice proves the **shape** end to end and falsifies the model; it does not,
and cannot yet, prove live acquisition.

### 7. The most newsworthy fact is the one the model correctly leaves blank

OKC has rich **configured access** data (109 agencies could pull its data) but
**observed use** — who actually ran searches — is unobtainable without records
requests. The §12.2 split (configured access vs observed use) handled this
correctly: observed searches land in **"what we don't know."** But that means the
single most important cell for accountability is empty for essentially every real
jurisdiction until records land. The design is *right*, and the result is
sobering: the honest dossier's headline is a gap.

### 8. Genuine contradictions are often several types at once

OKC's 299-vs-190 "how many cameras" disagreement is *both* a `value_disagreement`
and, underneath, a scope difference (metro vs city limits) that looks like a
`count_basis_mismatch`. The typed contradiction vocabulary forces a single label;
reality is layered. The dossier keeps **both figures visible without collapse**
(the defining standard, §3.1) — which is the important property — but the single
`contradiction_type` is a simplification the engine (P08) should revisit.

## Deferred / handed forward

- **Server-side PDF.** SIG-UI-013's print path is satisfied with print-CSS HTML
  (browser Print-to-PDF); a true server-side PDF renderer is **P15.2** (ADR-032).
- **The reconciliation engine.** This slice's `reconcile/{weight,counts,model}.py`
  is the minimal seed; **P08** supersedes and extends it (ADR-031).
- **The production dossier surface.** This renderer is minimal and deliberately
  un-gold-plated; **P15.2** supersedes it with the full epistemic visual language,
  a11y/no-JS, and editorial standards.

## Net assessment

The design **survived** falsification on its core claim — that competing counts
are *reconciled, not aggregated*, and that contradictions stay visible. It
**failed** on completeness (three count predicates missing), on an internal
inconsistency (Appendix D.2 vs §10.6), and on an unmodeled dimension (scope vs
basis) that real municipal data exposed immediately. None of these are fatal;
all are cheaper to fix now, at one jurisdiction, than at twenty thousand — which
is exactly why the vertical slice is a hard gate.
