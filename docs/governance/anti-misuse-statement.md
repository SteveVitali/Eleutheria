# Anti-misuse statement

*This is a first-class page. It adopts docs/2_canonical_design_spec.md §46.3
(SIG-GOV-019) and states SIG's position in SIG's own voice. It belongs on the
public methodology page. A project that hides from its hardest question is not
credible on any of its easier ones.*

## The tension, stated plainly

SIG will not pretend its hardest question does not exist. **Mapping surveillance
infrastructure does inherently make avoidance easier.** A map of where cameras
and readers are deployed is, unavoidably, also a map of where they are not. We
are not going to wave that away, and we are not going to bury it in a
disclaimer.

Here is where we stand.

**SIG's position is that public knowledge of publicly deployed infrastructure is
legitimate and necessary for democratic oversight; that the same information is
already available to anyone who drives the road and looks; and that the
alternative — infrastructure that watches the public while remaining unknown to
it — is the condition the project exists to remedy.**

The infrastructure SIG documents is installed in public, on public roads and
public buildings, by public agencies and their vendors, largely without public
debate. It watches people who never consented to being watched and mostly do not
know it is there. An asymmetry in which the watchers are known to no one and
everyone is known to the watchers is not a neutral state of affairs to be
preserved; it is precisely the imbalance that public-interest research and
journalism exist to correct.

## What this commits us to

Because we take the tension seriously rather than denying it, we bind ourselves
in the ways it demands. SIG **does not** build a real-time device-liveness feed,
a per-person lookup, an "is a camera watching me right now" surface, or
individual-officer tracking as a product (SIG-GOV-017; non-goals N1/N3). SIG
**does not** publish instructions for damaging, disabling, tampering with, or
evading enforcement in the commission of wrongdoing (SIG-GOV-018). Those are not
grudging concessions; they follow from the same principle that justifies the
project — that this is oversight of institutions, not a tool against people.

We would rather answer the hard question in public and lose the argument
honestly than win it by refusing to ask.
